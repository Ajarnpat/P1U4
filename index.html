Unit equations explained

Vector arrows matter. In one-dimensional problems, signs indicate direction. “Net” must refer to the external resultant force on the selected object/system.

Topic 4.1

p⃗ = m v⃗

Momentum of an object. m is mass (kg); v⃗ is velocity (m/s). Momentum p⃗ is a vector in kg·m/s.

Definition; provided on AP equation sheet.
Topic 4.2

Δp⃗ = p⃗ − p⃗0

Change in momentum = final minus initial vector momentum. Use components or signed 1D values.

Definition/relationship in CED.
Topic 4.2

F⃗net = Δp⃗ / Δt

For the interval, this is average net force. An instantaneous force is the tangent gradient of a p–t graph. For constant mass: F⃗net = m a⃗.

Provided relationship; constant-mass form F = ma.
Topic 4.2

J⃗net = F⃗avg,net Δt

Net impulse over Δt (s). F⃗avg,net is average net force (N); impulse has N·s.

Provided on AP equation sheet.
Topic 4.2

J⃗net = Δp⃗

Net impulse–momentum theorem. Also equals the signed area under Fnet versus time.

Provided on AP equation sheet.
Topic 4.3

p⃗system = Σ p⃗i

Add the momentum vectors of all objects selected as the system.

System model; vector sum.
Topic 4.3

p⃗system,0 = p⃗system

Only when net external force/impulse on the selected system is zero or negligible over the interval.

Conservation principle; conditional.
Topic 4.3

v⃗cm = (Σ p⃗i)/(Σ mi)

Velocity of the center of mass: total momentum divided by total mass.

Relevant CED equation.
Topic 4.4

K = ½mv²

Kinetic energy (J) of one translating object. Compare ΣK before and after to classify collisions.

Previously learned in Unit 3; AP sheet.
Scope note: Unit 4.3 treats conservation of momentum quantitatively in 1D and semi-quantitatively in 2D. The AP Physics 1 framework does not require solving simultaneous equations for 2D collisions.
