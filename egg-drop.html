A simplified impulse–momentum model. The egg travels downwards, then is brought to rest.

This model considers the average net force on the egg, not the contact force from the landing surface. During stopping, the surface force is larger than the net force because weight acts downward. It does not predict whether an egg breaks.

Predict, then test

Keep mass and arrival speed fixed. What happens to the average net force if stopping time doubles?

For the same Δp, |F_avg,net| = |Δp|/Δt. Doubling stopping time halves the magnitude of average net force. A softer landing surface can increase stopping time.

* Contact-force estimate assumes a vertical stop, upward average contact force and gravity g = 9.81 m/s². F_contact,avg = F_net,avg + mg. This is not a real egg fracture model.
