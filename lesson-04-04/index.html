Part 1 · Think first, reveal second

Topic 4.4 is short. You already explored much of it in 4.3. For each question, write your own reasoning before revealing the answer. These questions cover every 2026 CED essential knowledge statement, 4.4.A.1–4.4.A.5.

4.4.A.1 · Q1

For a collision to be classified as elastic, which energy quantity must be unchanged?

Think about the two-object system, not one object.
The total kinetic energy of the system must be the same before and after. Total momentum is also conserved when external impulse is negligible.
4.4.A.2 · Q2

Can cart A gain kinetic energy in an elastic collision? What must happen elsewhere in the system?

Consider transfers of energy between the two carts.
Yes. One cart can gain kinetic energy while the other loses the same amount; it is the total system kinetic energy that remains unchanged.
4.4.A.3 · Q3

A two-cart system has 12 J of kinetic energy before and 9 J after a collision. How would you classify it?

Compare the system totals, not the speeds.
Inelastic: the total kinetic energy decreases by 3 J.
4.4.A.4 · Q4

In that 12 J → 9 J collision, has 3 J of energy been destroyed? Explain.

Distinguish kinetic energy from all energy.
No. Energy is conserved. The 3 J reduction in kinetic energy corresponds to energy transferred to other forms (for example, deformation/internal energy and sound).
4.4.A.5 · Q5

What additional observation lets you call an inelastic collision perfectly inelastic?

Look at the final motion of the two objects.
The objects stick together and move with the same final velocity after the collision.
Apply · Q6

Does a reduction in kinetic energy mean momentum was not conserved?

Recall your results from Lesson 4.3.
No. With negligible external impulse, system momentum stays constant in elastic, partially inelastic and perfectly inelastic collisions.
Apply · Q7

If two objects stick together, must their shared final velocity be zero?

What determines the final momentum?
No. Their shared velocity is set by the initial total signed momentum divided by the combined mass.

Part 2 · Check your understanding (8 MCQs)

Choose an answer, then check it. Explain disagreements using a principle or equation.

MCQ 1 of 8

In an isolated collision, the system kinetic energy is 24 J before and 24 J after. Which statement is justified?

Choose one answer
Equal initial and final total kinetic energy defines an elastic collision.
MCQ 2 of 8

Which statement is true for an elastic collision?

Choose one answer
Only the total system kinetic energy must be unchanged; individual energies can be transferred.
MCQ 3 of 8

Two 1 kg carts: A initially moves at +4 m/s, B is at rest. After impact A stops and B moves at +4 m/s. The collision is…

Choose one answer
Initial and final system kinetic energies are each 8 J, and total momentum is conserved.
MCQ 4 of 8

A 2 kg cart moving at +3 m/s sticks to a stationary 1 kg cart. What is their shared final speed?

Choose one answer
By momentum conservation, (2 kg)(3 m/s) = (3 kg)v, so v = +2 m/s.
MCQ 5 of 8

For the sticking collision in Question 4, how much kinetic energy is transformed into other forms?

Choose one answer
K before = 9 J; K after = 6 J; the reduction is 3 J.
MCQ 6 of 8

An isolated system has 60 J of kinetic energy before and 45 J afterwards. Which conclusion is correct?

Choose one answer
Total energy is conserved. A reduction in kinetic energy indicates an inelastic collision; it need not involve sticking.
MCQ 7 of 8

Two objects rebound after a collision, and the total kinetic energy falls from 10 J to 8 J. This collision is…

Choose one answer
The total kinetic energy decreases, but the objects separate rather than stick.
MCQ 8 of 8

Identical 1 kg carts: A initially moves at +4 m/s, B at rest. They stick together. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy remains as kinetic energy afterwards?

Choose one answer
The shared final speed is +2 m/s; K before = 8 J and K after = 4 J.

FRQ 1 · One collision, two possible outcomes

A 1.00 kg cart A moves right at 4.00 m/s. A 1.00 kg cart B is initially at rest on a level track. External horizontal impulse is negligible. In trial X, the final velocities are vA,f = 0 and vB,f = +4.00 m/s. In trial Y, the final velocities are vA,f = +1.00 m/s and vB,f = +3.00 m/s.

(a) State a principle and verify whether momentum is conserved in each trial.

(b) Starting from the relevant energy equation, calculate the initial total kinetic energy and the final total kinetic energy in both trials.

(c) Classify each collision, justifying your answer numerically.

(d) In trial X, explain how cart B can gain kinetic energy even though the collision is elastic.
